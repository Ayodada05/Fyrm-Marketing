PIPEDA, in plain language.

Incorporating a business means trusting us with personal information. Canada's federal privacy law sets the rules for how we handle it — here is how we follow them.

What PIPEDA is

PIPEDA — the Personal Information Protection and Electronic Documents Act — is Canada's federal privacy law. It governs how private-sector organizations collect, use, and disclose personal information in the course of commercial activity.

Incorporating and maintaining a company involves personal information: the names and addresses of directors, officers, and shareholders, and the details we need to prepare and submit government filings. That makes PIPEDA central to how Fyrm works. Our privacy policy describes what we collect and why; this page explains the principles behind those practices.

PIPEDA is built on ten fair information principles. Here is what each one means at Fyrm.

1. Accountability

We are responsible for the personal information under our control. Fyrm Technologies Inc. assigns responsibility for privacy compliance internally, and we hold ourselves to the commitments on this page and in our privacy policy.

2. Identifying purposes

We identify why we are collecting personal information at or before the time we collect it. When we ask for a director's address, it is because a filing requires it — and we say so.

3. Consent

We collect, use, and disclose personal information with your knowledge and consent, except where the law permits or requires otherwise. You may withdraw consent at any time, subject to legal or contractual restrictions and reasonable notice — though some withdrawals will limit what we can file on your behalf.

4. Limiting collection

We collect only the personal information we need for the purposes we have identified, and we collect it fairly and lawfully. If a question is not needed for your incorporation or an ongoing filing, we do not ask it.

5. Limiting use, disclosure, and retention

We use and disclose personal information only for the purposes it was collected for — chiefly preparing and submitting filings to Corporations Canada and the provincial and territorial registries — unless you consent to more or the law requires it. We retain information only as long as those purposes and our legal obligations require.

6. Accuracy

Filings are only as good as the information behind them, so we keep personal information as accurate, complete, and up to date as its purposes require — and you can review and correct your information at any time.

7. Safeguards

We protect personal information with safeguards appropriate to its sensitivity. Our security page describes our approach.

8. Openness

We are open about our policies and practices for managing personal information. Our privacy policy is written in plain language, and this page is part of that same commitment.

9. Individual access

On request, we will tell you what personal information we hold about you, how we use it, and to whom it has been disclosed, and we will give you access to it. If something is inaccurate or incomplete, we will correct it.

10. Challenging compliance

You can challenge our compliance with any of these principles by reaching us through our contact page. We look into every concern, and if you are not satisfied with our response, you may also contact the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.
