FAQ

Answers before you hit "file".

Everything founders ask us about incorporating and staying compliant in Canada — grouped so you can find your answer fast.

Getting started

How long does incorporation take?

Most founders finish in about twenty minutes and are incorporated within a day. Anything past that is up to the registry — and we keep you posted at every step.

What do I need on hand to start?

Just the basics: the name you have in mind (or none — we run the name search), who the directors and shareholders will be, their addresses, and where in Canada you want to incorporate. We ask plain-language questions and take it from there.

Do I need a lawyer to incorporate?

Most straightforward incorporations don't need one. Fyrm is not a law firm and doesn't provide legal advice — if your situation is complex, we recommend speaking with a professional.

Can I use Fyrm anywhere in Canada?

Yes. We file with Corporations Canada and all 13 provincial & territorial registries — wherever you incorporate, the paperwork is our problem.

Sole proprietorship vs. corporation

What's the difference?

A sole proprietorship is just you — you and the business are legally the same, so its debts and obligations are personally yours. A corporation is a separate legal entity: it owns its assets, carries its own obligations, and keeps existing independently of its owners. That separation is the main reason founders incorporate.

When does incorporating make sense?

There's no single right moment. Founders often incorporate when they want to separate business obligations from personal ones, bring on partners, sign contracts under a company name, or simply look established to clients. If you're weighing it for your own situation, a professional advisor can help — we're not one, and we don't give legal advice.

Can I start as a sole proprietor and incorporate later?

Generally, yes. Many businesses begin as sole proprietorships and incorporate once things pick up. When you're ready, Fyrm handles the whole move — name search, incorporation, filings, and your minute book, from one dashboard.

Incorporation

Should I incorporate federally or provincially?

In plain terms: federal incorporation protects your company name across Canada and suits businesses that operate in more than one province; provincial or territorial incorporation ties the company to where you actually operate. Both create a real corporation — you choose the jurisdiction, and we file with Corporations Canada or your chosen provincial or territorial registry.

What's a named company vs. a numbered company?

A named company uses a name you choose, which needs a registry name search before it's approved. A numbered company simply uses the number the registry assigns — quicker to set up, and many later register a business name to operate under. Either works; we run the search and handle the filing.

What documents do I get?

Your certificate and articles of incorporation, plus a complete minute book — by-laws, share registers, and director resolutions — all in your Fyrm dashboard. Every government filing we make afterwards lands there too.

Staying compliant

What filings does a corporation owe after incorporating?

Corporations file an annual return with their registry, plus change filings when details move — directors, registered office, and the like. The specifics vary by jurisdiction; Fyrm tracks exactly what your corporation owes and files it on time.

What happens if I miss a filing deadline?

Registries can penalize corporations that fall behind, and one that stays out of compliance long enough can eventually be dissolved. That's exactly what Fyrm exists to prevent — your deadlines are tracked so you never miss a filing again.

How does Fyrm keep me compliant?

Deadlines are tracked in your dashboard, government filings are prepared and submitted for you, and your minute book updates with every filing — so your corporation stays in good standing while you run the business.
